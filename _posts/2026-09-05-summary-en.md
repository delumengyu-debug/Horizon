## [CVE-2026-85046: Chromium V8 Sandbox RCE Exploited in the Wild](https://nvd.nist.gov/vuln/detail/cve-2026-85046) ⭐️ 9.0/10

CVE-2026-85046 is an actively exploited type confusion vulnerability in Chromium's V8 JavaScript engine that enables a sandbox escape and remote code execution. It affects all Chromium versions prior to the latest stable release, Chrome .82, which shipped two days ago. This is severe because it affects virtually every Chromium-based browser, including Chrome, Edge, and others, with active exploitation in the wild. Users and enterprises need to update immediately to the patched version to prevent potential system compromise. The root cause is a type confusion bug in the V8 engine that, combined with a sandbox escape, allows arbitrary code execution. Google reportedly paid a researcher $1,000 for the report, a sum that commenters note is far below the vulnerability's actual market value.

hackernews · negura · Sep 4, 21:52 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49570669)

**Background**: V8 is Google's open-source high-performance JavaScript and WebAssembly engine, written in C++ and used in Chrome, Chromium, and Node.js, among others. A sandbox escape vulnerability occurs when an attacker who has already compromised a child process (e.g., via a V8 bug) breaks out of the browser's security sandbox to gain higher privileges on the underlying operating system. Such issues are critical because modern browsers rely on sandboxing as a key defense against malicious web content.

## [Anthropic Researchers Formalize Fermat's Last Theorem in Lean](https://www.anthropic.com/research/formalizing-fermats-last-theorem) ⭐️ 9.0/10

Anthropic researchers have formalized Fermat's Last Theorem in the Lean proof assistant, completing a proof that follows the Darmon–Diamond–Taylor 1995 exposition of the Wiles–Taylor–Wiles argument. The formalization was reportedly produced at remarkable speed, signaling a new stage in AI-assisted mathematics. This milestone shows that large-scale mathematical formalization is now feasible, which could help catch errors in existing proofs and reduce the burden of refereeing new mathematics. It also demonstrates that LLMs can be grounded in rigorous, machine-checked reasoning through tools like Lean. According to community discussion, the formalized proof is not the modern proof, but rather the 1995 Darmon–Diamond–Taylor exposition of the Wiles–Taylor–Wiles argument, using the Langlands–Tunnell theorem and Ribet's level-lowering theorem. Kevin Buzzard's blog post provides context on what the accomplishment does and does not mean.

hackernews · jlebar · Sep 4, 18:42 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49568506)

**Background**: Lean is an open-source proof assistant and functional programming language based on the Calculus of Inductive Constructions, which allows mathematicians to write definitions and theorems that are machine-checked for correctness. Formal proof translates mathematical reasoning into a formal language so that every step is logically verified by the computer, unlike informal paper proofs. Fermat's Last Theorem, stating that no three positive integers a, b, c satisfy a^n + b^n = c^n for n > 2, was famously proved by Andrew Wiles in 1994–1995. Formalizing such a deep and lengthy proof is an area of ongoing research in mathematical formalization.

## [Scientists Complete Second Full Map of a Fruit Fly Brain](https://arstechnica.com/science/2026/09/brain-of-a-male-fly-completely-mapped-joins-earlier-map-of-a-female-brain/) ⭐️ 9.0/10

Researchers have finished reconstructing a complete connectome of a male fruit fly brain, making it the second full fly brain map after an earlier female connectome. The achievement means every neuron and neural connection in the fly brain has now been mapped twice, once in each sex. This is significant because having two complete fly-brain connectomes allows scientists to compare male and female neural wiring directly. Such comparative connectomics can reveal how sex-specific circuits are organized and will deepen our understanding of how brains produce behavior. The new map is a male Drosophila melanogaster connectome produced at the level of individual neurons and their connections, complementing the previously mapped female brain. At this scale, connectomes are typically reconstructed from electron microscopy data and represent a wiring diagram of every neuron and synapse.

rss · ArsTechnica · Sep 4, 16:24

**Background**: A connectome is a comprehensive map of neural connections in the brain, often described as its wiring diagram. Connectomes can be coarse maps of brain regions or detailed maps of individual neurons; mapping individual neurons typically requires electron microscopy at nanometer resolution. So far, complete neural connectomes are available only for small animals such as the roundworm C. elegans and Drosophila melanogaster, as well as small regions of mammalian brains.
